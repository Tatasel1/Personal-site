Who am I?

My name is Tatasel Daniel-Valentin and I am an Computer Engineering student at Universitatea Nationala de Stiinte si Tehnologie Politehnica Bucuresti

I am 20 years old, currently living in Bucharest, Romania due to my Univesity being here, but I was born and raised in Calfat, a city near Romania's border with Bulgaria. I've always been fascinated with all things technical, ever since I was little I used to take apart old computers and put them back toghether again.

Those moments are where it all started, where my passion and curiosity for how everything works, what is behind the tools we so commonly use in our daily lives, ignited. This hunger for learning, for developing solutions and coming up with ideas is what motivated me throughout my entire life.

Follow me, as I will show you the insides of my life, my hobbies and also the things I have been working on, my so called "projects".

An engineer's mindset

To me, engineering isn't just a major; it's a way of thinking. I'm wired to optimize—I don't just want to build things that work, I want to build them to work efficiently. It's a drive that pushes me deep into C and C++, where you're forced to be deliberate about memory and data structures to create genuinely fast, resource-conscious solutions.

But precision is only half the equation. That same mindset applies to building scalable architecture with C# or using Python to automate a tedious process. It all comes back to finding a smarter, cleaner way to solve a problem, and I track all of it using Git across both Linux and Windows.

The other half of that equation, which I learned firsthand in LSAC and Interact, is people. The most elegant code in the world doesn't mean much if it's built in a silo. My volunteer work taught me that clear communication and taking real responsibility for deadlines are just as critical as any technical skill. It's the same lesson I practice on the volleyball court—you win through trust and perfect synchronization, not just individual talent. At the end of the day, that's my entire approach: know the tools intimately, but always prioritize the team you're building with.
