Who am I?

I'm Tatasel Daniel-Valentin, a Computer Engineering student at Universitatea Nationala de Stiinte si Tehnologie Politehnica Bucuresti. I'm originally from Calafat, a small city on the border Romania has with Bulgaria, but I'm currently based in Bucharest for my studies.

My fascination with tech isn't new at all. It all started when I was a kid, taking apart old family computers just to see how they ticked. I was hooked on figuring out what, exactly, was going on behind the screen.

That same curiosity is what drives me today. It's a hunger to understand the "why" behind the "what," and it's the force that motivates me to study in order to later develop solutions and build new things. It’s the core of my "engineer's mindset."

An engineer's mindset

To me, engineering isn't just a major; it's a way of thinking. I'm wired to optimize—I don't just want to build things that work, I want to build them to work efficiently. It's a drive that pushes me deep into C and C++, where you're forced to be deliberate about memory and data structures to create genuinely fast, resource-conscious solutions.

But precision is only half the equation. That same mindset applies to building scalable architecture with C# or using Python to automate a tedious process. It all comes back to finding a smarter, cleaner way to solve a problem, and I track all of it using Git across both Linux and Windows.

The other half of that equation, which I learned firsthand in LSAC and Interact, is people. The most elegant code in the world doesn't mean much if it's built in a silo. My volunteer work taught me that clear communication and taking real responsibility for deadlines are just as critical as any technical skill. It's the same lesson I practice on the volleyball court—you win through trust and perfect synchronization, not just individual talent. At the end of the day, that's my entire approach: know the tools intimately, but always prioritize the team you're building with.

Hobbies and Interests

When I'm not deep in project or class work, I need to get away from the screen. My go-to is volleyball. It's how I stay active, but it’s also a constant reminder of how important teamwork and strategy are—which applies just as much to engineering as it does to a game.

I've also been fascinated with cars for as long as I can remember. It's not just the mechanics; it's the art of the design, the sound of the engine, and that feeling of freedom on an open road. It’s another form of complex, beautiful engineering.

Finally, I have a constant itch to travel. Just exploring a new environment and walking down unfamiliar streets is the best way for me to clear my head and get a fresh perspective.

My Goals

Looking ahead, my goal is pretty simple: I want to use my engineering skills to build cool things that actually matter. I thrive in fast-paced environments where I can team up with smart people, keep learning, and be part of pushing technology forward.

Right now, I'm focused on finishing my degree strong, but my main priority is getting as much hands-on experience as I can through internships and my own projects. Down the line, I'd love to grow into a leadership role where I can help drive new advancements in areas like AI, cybersecurity, or complex software development.

Ultimately, my ambition is to be in the thick of it—using my skills to tackle complex challenges and build tech that genuinely improves lives.

Things I've Built

This is where the theory hits the keyboard. My "engineer's mindset" is all about building, so here are a couple of my favorite projects where I got to put those ideas into practice.

An Educational Pac-Man

For this team project, we built a Pac-Man-style game in Unity, but with a twist. We added a pop-quiz component to make it educational. I was responsible for writing the C# scripts that handled all the player movement, collisions, and menus. It was a really fun way to blend classic gaming with a bit of learning.

Tech used: Mostly C# (both in Unity and for the quiz logic)

Automatic Plant-Watering Robot

I built this little guy for the NextLabTech competition. It’s a robot designed to automate plant watering (so you don't kill them!). I used an Arduino UNO, hooked it up to a humidity sensor, and wrote the code to make it automatically water the plant whenever the soil got too dry. No more sad, thirsty plants!

Tech used: C++ in the Arduino IDE
